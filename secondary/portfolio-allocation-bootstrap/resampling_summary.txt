This solution estimates the minimum-variance allocation between two assets using the sample variances and covariance of 100 paired daily return observations. The model chooses the fraction alpha invested in Asset 1, with 1 - alpha invested in Asset 2, by minimizing the portfolio variance. Since Asset 1 has lower sample variance than Asset 2 and the two assets have almost zero/slightly negative correlation, the fitted minimum-variance portfolio places more weight on Asset 1 while still keeping some Asset 2 for diversification.

The plug-in estimate is alpha_hat = 0.706418, so the portfolio invests about 70.6% in Asset 1 and 29.4% in Asset 2. To measure uncertainty, I used a nonparametric bootstrap with 50000 paired-row resamples from the empirical distribution function. The 95% percentile bootstrap confidence interval is [0.612544, 0.791623], meaning the plausible minimum-variance weight on Asset 1 is roughly 61.3% to 79.2%.
